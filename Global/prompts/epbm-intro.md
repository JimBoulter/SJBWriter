# Easy Peasy Book Machine (EPBM)

**From an idea to a finished novel, one step at a time.**

EPBM is a collection of AI writing prompts adapted from material shared by Lynn and the Future Fiction Academy. It can help you develop a story from its first premise through the final draft, while keeping you in control of the creative decisions.

**Getting started:** Give the AI a genre and any ideas you already have, then select the **Premise** prompt. A genre is all you need to begin. Review the suggested premises, choose one, and make it your own before moving on.

## The Writing Process

1. **Premise:** Generate story ideas and choose your direction.
2. **Characters:** Develop the people who will bring your story to life.
3. **Outline:** Build the story's structure. Review and revise it until you're happy with the direction.
4. **Chapter Plan:** Plan the next chapter using your outline and previous writing.
5. **Draft:** Write the chapter's first prose draft.
6. **Edit Plan:** Review the draft and identify improvements.
7. **Final Draft:** Revise the chapter using the Edit Plan.
8. **Continuity Check:** Check consistency with earlier chapters and the established story, without revealing future events.
9. **Review and Refine:** Evaluate the manuscript as a whole, or pause every few chapters to make sure the story is working.

**Steps 4–8 form the chapter loop.** Repeat them for each chapter, updating the chapter number and selecting the appropriate context. SJB Writer doesn't advance these prompts automatically; you decide when each step is ready.

The AI can do much of the writing, but you're still the author. Change the plan, rewrite a scene, or take the story in an entirely new direction whenever you like.

## Making It Your Own

The EPBM prompts are genre-neutral, but your stories don't have to be. You can add specific instructions for genre, tone, style, audience, or anything else you want the AI to consider. You can also include a Genre Writing Guide to help shape the results.

When you select a prompt, it appears in the Instruction field below the Editor. You can add your own directions there without changing the saved prompt. For longer reference material, such as a Genre Writing Guide, you can create a file and select it as Context.

The more clearly you communicate what you want, the more useful your AI assistant can be.

## Instructions and Context

Think of **Instructions** as what you're asking the AI to do right now, and **Context** as the information it needs to do it well.

When you select an EPBM prompt, it appears in the Instruction field below the Editor. You can add your own directions there, such as a genre, intended audience, tone, or anything specific you want from that generation. Changing the Instruction field doesn't change your saved prompt.

**Background** is where you keep the information your story depends on: premises, characters, outlines, research, and even Genre Writing Guides. Select the files you want the AI to use as Context. You can also select previous chapters from Writing to help maintain continuity.

You don't need to send everything every time. Choose the material relevant to the task, and remember that the more context you provide, the more the AI has to work through.

**A simple rule:** Tell the AI what you want in Instructions. Give it what it needs in Context.

## Before you begin the chapter loop

Review your premise, characters, and outline until you're satisfied with the story's direction. These become the foundation for the chapters that follow. You can still make changes later, but updating these documents will help keep your writing consistent.

## Keeping Your Story on Track

As your novel grows, the AI needs to know both what you've planned and what you've already written.

For the first few chapters, you can usually provide the complete previous chapters as Context. As the manuscript grows, consider using a synopsis of earlier chapters along with the most recent 3–4 chapters in full. Include your premise, characters, and outline as needed.

This gives the AI enough history to maintain continuity without overwhelming it with the entire manuscript.

**Remember: The outline is the plan. The manuscript is what actually happened.**

Sometimes the story takes a better direction than you originally intended. That's fine! If you decide to keep a change, update your outline and other affected background material so future generations follow the story you're actually writing, not an outdated plan.

## Organizing Your Chapters

You can keep each stage of a chapter in its own file, or combine the Chapter Plan, Draft, and Edit Plan into one working file. Whatever works best for you!

Once you're satisfied with the Final Draft, consider keeping it in a separate file. That gives you a clean manuscript to use as context for future chapters, without repeatedly including earlier plans and editing notes.

As you move to the next chapter, select the relevant Final Drafts rather than the working files. The AI needs to know what made it into the story, not every idea you considered along the way.
