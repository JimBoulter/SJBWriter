## Premise:
This is a short fantasy about a giant egg found by a pre-teen girl walking home from school through the forrest who decides to keep it and raise whatever is in this massive egg, about the size of a watermelon, as a pet, without thinking about what could be in an egg so large. It was just another normal Tuesday. In the short act 1, she finds the egg, nearly drops and breaks it a few times carrying it home in her backpack. She has to leave her books behind to make space in her bag, but it is worth it. She gets home and hides it from her parents in her bedroom under a blanket in a warm corner where it finishes incubating in just a few days. She wakes up on Saturday to discover that the egg was for a dragon, which is now looking to her for its first meal. It has already stretched out from the egg to be as tall as her head when sitting on its back end, and its wing span is enough to fully cover her bed. She spends the weekend tending to the small beast.

**Setting:**

* The story takes place in a small, rural town surrounded by a dense and vibrant forest.
* The main character lives in a cozy suburban home with a small backyard and a roomy bedroom.
* The town has a moderate pace of life, with kids going to school and teenagers spending their free time exploring the woods or hanging out with friends.

**Main Character:**

* The protagonist is Mary, a 12 year old girl, who is curious, adventurous, and loves nature.
* She's a bit of a daydreamer and often gets lost in her own thoughts while walking home from school.
* She's also responsible and caring, as evidenced by her decision to take the egg home and care for it.
* She calls her parents Mamma and Poppa, and we may not learn their names until they are present when she interacts with the people of the village in Act 2.

**The Egg:**

* The giant egg is a mysterious, ancient artifact that's been hidden in the forest for centuries.
* It's enormous, for an egg, with dimensions that are hard to comprehend. It clearly isn't a chicken. Nor a reptile, like an alligator. Nor anything she could even imagine.
* When the girl finds it, it's still intact and seems to be in a state of dormancy.

**The Dragon:**

* When the girl discovers that the egg is a dragon, she's shocked and amazed.
* The dragon is still small, but will soon start growing at an incredible rate, and its presence is both thrilling and terrifying.
* The dragon is hungry and looks to the girl for its first meal, which creates tension and raises questions about the girl's decision to care for it. Fortunately, the family raises chickens. A full chicken every two days turns out to be perfect, but only for about the first month. Larger meals later will allow it to eat only once a week through this story. It will eventually move up to still larger meals and slow to eating once a month after a bout a year.

**Conflict:**

* The main conflict of Act 1 is the girl's discovery of the dragon and her subsequent struggle to care for it.
* As the dragon grows and becomes more demanding, the girl must navigate the challenges of raising a wild creature in her home.
* This sets the stage for the rest of the story, as the girl must confront the consequences of her actions and figure out how to balance her desire to care for the dragon with her own needs and the needs of those around her.

**Themes:**

* The story touches on themes of responsibility, curiosity, and the power of unexpected events.
* The girl's decision to care for the dragon forces her to confront her own limitations and the challenges of taking care of a wild creature.
* The story also hints at the idea that the dragon is not just a pet, but a wild animal with its own needs and desires.
