# 3.2 - Prompting

**Now let's write something bigger!**

You've generated a poem with a simple instruction. But AI can help with almost every part of writing a book, from developing a premise to drafting the final chapter.

Let's give it enough information to write an actual story.

There are three parts to this process:

- **Instruction:** What you want the AI to do.
- **Context:** What the AI needs to know.
- **Output:** Where you want the writing to appear.

## 1. Choose a Prompt

Look in the **Prompts** pane on the right, under AI Setup. You'll find **EPBM** (Easy Peasy Book Machine), a collection of writing prompts adapted from material shared by Lynn and the Future Fiction Academy.

Expand EPBM and click the **selection button** beside **Chapter Draft**.

The prompt will appear automatically in the **Instruction** field below the Editor.

Clicking a prompt's name instead opens it for editing. You can create and customize your own prompts later.

For now, find **Chapter X** in the Instruction field and change it to **Chapter 1**. This won't change the saved prompt, only the instruction you're using right now.

## 2. Add Context

The AI needs to know what story it's writing!

I've prepared a sample story about a young girl who discovers a dragon egg. You'll find its premise, characters, outline, and Chapter 1 plan in **3.3 - The Dragon Egg Story**, under Background.

Click the **selection button** beside that file. Its contents will be sent to the AI as context.

You can select multiple Background and Writing files this way. That's how you give the AI information about your characters, previous chapters, and anything else it needs to maintain continuity.

## 3. Choose Where to Write

Create a new file called **Chapter 1** in the Writing pane and open it in the Editor.

Click in the blank Editor to position your cursor, then click **Generate** or use **Command-Return (Mac) / Ctrl-Enter (Windows)**.

This may take a little longer than your pineapple-dragon poem. You're asking for at least 1,500 words of actual story!

## Congratulations!

You've now used a saved prompt, supplied story context, and generated a chapter draft.

Read it. Change it. Improve it. Or tell the AI to try something different. You are still the author, and the final decisions are yours.

If you enjoyed Mary's story, the outline already includes fourteen more chapters you can explore. Or start a new project and bring your own world to life.

**Your stories. Your worlds. Your words.**

Now go write something!
